Our paper describing the open-source packages I develop for photonics is now published in *Mathematics*, and it is open access.

Designing nanophotonic devices means solving Maxwell's equations many times, and gradient-based optimization needs the derivative of a figure of merit with respect to thousands of design variables. Writing those derivatives by hand (the adjoint method) is tedious and error-prone. The tools of machine learning changed that: automatic differentiation computes them for us, with only small changes to the forward solver.

The paper presents three Python codes built on this idea:

- [gyptis](https://gyptis.gitlab.io), a finite element solver with a high-level interface for problems common in photonics (scattering, gratings, modal analysis)
- [nannos](https://nannos.gitlab.io), a Fourier modal method for multilayer bi-periodic metasurfaces
- [protis](https://protis.gitlab.io), a plane wave expansion method for band diagrams of two-dimensional photonic crystals

All three support automatic differentiation, so the same code that simulates a device can also optimize it. The paper walks through topology optimization examples with each of them. The figure above shows one: dielectric scatterers optimized to maximize scattering at 600 nm, for each polarization.

Metamaterials are artificial materials made of subwavelength inclusions, with electromagnetic properties not found in nature. During our work on infrared multispectral imaging, we used modal analysis to understand resonant metamaterial filters, then designed, fabricated and measured them.

## Metal insulator metal resonant filters

We carried out a modal analysis of metal–insulator–metal (MIM) metamaterials in the far infrared. These structures act as resonant reflection bandcut filters, independent of the polarization and direction of incidence. We showed that the resonant reflection dip comes from the excitation of quasimodes (modes associated with a complex frequency), which leads to quasi-total absorption.

We fabricated large-area samples made of chromium nanorod gratings on top of Si/Cr layers on a silicon substrate. Fourier transform spectrophotometry measurements agree well with finite element simulations. A quasimodal expansion gives a minimal resonant model that fits the full-wave simulations and reveals the excitation conditions of the modes.

## Annular aperture metasurfaces

We studied the diffractive behavior of arrays of square coaxial apertures in a gold layer. Their resonant transmission enhancement makes tunable bandpass filters for multispectral imaging in the 7–13 μm range. A modal analysis guided the design and showed that the resonance peak comes from the excitation of leaky modes of the open photonic structure. FTIR transmission measurements of samples on silicon agree well with the numerical results and show an angular tolerance of the fabricated filters up to 30°.

## Silicon Mie resonators

Subwavelength dielectric Mie resonators combine the advantages of dielectric microstructures and of metallic nanoparticles supporting surface plasmons. We fabricated monocrystalline silicon resonators over large scales by spontaneous dewetting of commercial silicon-on-insulator substrates. The resonators feature two resonant modes in the visible spectrum, and assisting the dewetting with electron beam lithography or focused ion beam patterning controls their size, position and arrangement, down to complex multimers.

## Invisible inhomogeneous media

We proposed a general method to manipulate at will an electromagnetic wave propagating in a two-dimensional medium, without introducing any scattering. It yields a whole class of isotropic, spatially varying permittivity and permeability profiles that stay invisible while shaping the field magnitude and phase. A metamaterial structure working in the infrared demonstrates deep subwavelength control of the electric field amplitude with a strong reduction of scattering. This offers an alternative route to invisibility with isotropic materials, and to tailoring the propagation of light at the nanoscale.

We then extended the method to cylindrical incident waves. Separating the complex wave solution into amplitude and phase controls both independently, without resorting to transformation optics or its quasi-conformal approximations, and leads to a general class of scattering-free solutions in isotropic, inhomogeneous media.

## Flat Luneburg lens

Graded-index profiles also make compact lenses. We designed a simplified flat Luneburg lens with transformation electromagnetics and fabricated it by casting ceramic composites. With only six layers and a highest permittivity of 12, it scans ±30° with gains of at least 18 dBi from 57 to 66 GHz, and carried raw high-definition video over a wireless link at scan angles up to 36°.
